# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Self-hosters of all types: solo developers and indie hackers running personal projects, small SaaS teams of 2-10 people tracking product metrics, and privacy-conscious organizations replacing Google Analytics due to GDPR or data sovereignty requirements. They open the dashboard with intent — a question in mind — and need the interface to help them find the answer. Some glance at numbers on a timer; many drill into filters, compare periods, and investigate causes.

## Product Purpose

Siraaj is a privacy-first, self-hosted web analytics platform. It gives users ownership of their data without cookies or consent banners. It exists as a lightweight, self-contained alternative to Google Analytics: simpler to understand, easier to trust, and fast enough to use daily. Success means the user opens the dashboard, finds what they were looking for, and closes it with clarity.

## Brand Personality

Warm precision. Friendly without being cute. Approachable without dumbing down. Like a trusted tool that respects your intelligence and saves your time. Three words: **clear, calm, honest**.

## Anti-references

- Google Analytics: overwhelming nested menus, too many report types, cognitive overload that punishes casual users
- Generic SaaS dashboards: identical blue card grids, hero metrics in boxes, interchangeable with every other SaaS product on the market
- Forced dark mode aesthetic: dark just to look "developer-y", not because it serves the user

## Design Principles

1. **Clarity before completeness** — every piece of information shown should earn its place. If it doesn't help the user answer a question, it shouldn't be on the screen.
2. **Drill-down without getting lost** — the dashboard supports deep investigation. Filters, comparisons, and drill-through must feel navigable, not disorienting. Users should always know where they are.
3. **Friendly to all levels** — a solo dev and a non-technical founder should both feel at home. Avoid jargon in labels; use plain language. Don't over-explain to experts, but don't exclude newcomers.
4. **Data is the hero** — the interface surfaces data, not itself. UI chrome should be minimal and recede. The numbers, charts, and trends are the product.
5. **Honest by default** — never obscure uncertainty or make numbers look better than they are. Bots, low-confidence data, and empty states should be transparent, not hidden.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance target: 4.5:1 contrast ratio for text, full keyboard navigation, visible focus indicators. Dark and light mode support. Motion should be optional (respect `prefers-reduced-motion`).
